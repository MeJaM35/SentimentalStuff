Brand Kit: SentimentalStuff

1. Brand Identity & Vibe

The Concept: SentimentalStuff is a sentiment analysis tool that feels less like a sterile B2B SaaS dashboard and more like a modern, insightful workspace. We are combining the quiet confidence of minimalist architecture with unexpected, bold flair to show that analyzing emotions shouldn't be devoid of them.

The Vibe:

Professional, yet Human: We handle data seriously, but we acknowledge the messy, emotional reality of the text we analyze.

Minimalist Foundation: Clean grids, generous white space (negative space), and structured layouts. No floating 3D blobs or generic tech illustrations.

Bold Flair: We use a striking, unexpected accent color and expressive typography for key moments (like sentiment scores or data visualizations) to break up the clinical feel.

2. Color Palette

We are avoiding the standard "SaaS Blue" or "Hacker Neon." Instead, we use a grounded, natural neutral palette, punctuated by one vibrant, emotional accent.

Background (The Canvas): Cloud White (#F8F9FA) - Not stark white, but a soft, readable off-white to reduce eye strain during long analysis sessions.

Primary Text & Borders (The Structure): Dark Slate (#1A1C20) - A rich, very dark grey. Softer than pure black, providing professional contrast without harshness.

Secondary Text (The Details): Cool Grey (#6B7280) - Used for metadata, small UI elements, and non-vital information.

The Bold Flair (The Accent): Electric Indigo (#4F46E5) - A vivid, energetic blue-purple. This is used sparingly but with high impact. It highlights the "Analyze" button, primary active states, and the most critical data points in the dashboard.

Sentiment Indicators (The Data):

Positive: Sage Green (#10B981) - Calm, organic.

Neutral: Sandstone (#D1D5DB) - Quiet, unobtrusive.

Negative: Terracotta (#EF4444) - Earthy, warm, but clear.

3. Typography

Typography does the heavy lifting in this minimalist design. We use a high-contrast pairing: a clean, geometric sans-serif for the UI and a character-rich serif for human-centric elements (like the summary text).

UI, Navigation, & Data (The System): Inter or Geist Sans

Why: Highly legible at small sizes, perfect for dashboards, KPIs, and tabular data. It represents the "machine" side of the analysis.

Usage: Bold for metric numbers, Regular/Medium for labels and buttons.

Headlines, Summaries, & Quotes (The Human Element): Playfair Display or Instrument Serif

Why: These fonts have distinct, elegant character. Using a serif for the AI-generated "Conversation Summary" or the actual uploaded text visually separates the content from the interface. It brings the "flair."

Usage: Only used for large page headers, the AI summary box, and displaying the raw transcript.

4. Visual Elements & UI Styling

Shapes: Sharp, crisp corners (0px to 4px border radius max). We want a structured, editorial feel, like a well-designed magazine, not a bubbly, rounded app.

Shadows: Extremely subtle. We rely on thin, 1px Dark Slate (at 10% opacity) borders to separate dashboard cards, rather than heavy drop shadows. This keeps the interface flat and modern.

Data Visualization (Charts):

Minimalist lines.

No gradients in the charts; use solid, flat colors from our sentiment palette.

Generous padding around all charts.

Empty States/Upload Area: Instead of generic cloud upload icons, use bold, large typography. E.g., a massive, elegant serif phrase saying "Drop a conversation here." surrounded by a thin, dotted border.

5. Brand Voice & Microcopy

Tone: Observant, precise, and subtly empathetic.

Rule: Avoid generic SaaS jargon ("Unlock your data's potential!"). Use concrete, slightly poetic language.

Examples:

Instead of "Upload File", use: "Read Transcript"

Instead of "Processing...", use: "Analyzing sentiment..."

Instead of "Dashboard", use: "Insights Workspace"
